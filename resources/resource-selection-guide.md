# Choosing a resource or provider

A directory listing is only a starting point. Verify that a service fits the child's age, location, needs, and safety concerns before sharing medical information or scheduling care.

## Identify the service you need

- **Pediatric primary care:** first review of growth, hydration, symptoms, development, medication, and referrals.
- **Pediatric dietitian:** nutrition and growth-related assessment and individualized nutrition planning.
- **Feeding/swallowing SLP:** chewing, oral-motor, swallowing, and feeding-skill concerns.
- **Feeding-trained occupational therapist:** sensory, motor, posture, utensils, routines, and participation within the therapist's scope.
- **Psychologist, psychiatrist, or eating-disorder clinician:** ARFID-related fear, low interest in eating, anxiety, distress, and mental-health treatment within their training.
- **Pediatric feeding or ARFID program:** coordinated care when multiple domains need assessment; Boston Children's Hospital is one example of this model.[2]
- **School team:** educational evaluation and daily accommodations; school processes are jurisdiction-specific.

ASHA distinguishes ARFID from pediatric feeding and swallowing disorders and describes interprofessional collaboration as the preferred practice pattern for pediatric feeding and swallowing care.[1] Pediatric clinical guidance also describes multidisciplinary management for ARFID.[3]

## Questions to ask before scheduling

- Do you work with children this age?
- Do you specifically see restricted eating, ARFID, pediatric feeding disorder, or swallowing concerns?
- Which parts do you assess, and which concerns require another professional?
- How do you protect safe foods, hydration, and the child's dignity?
- Do you use pressure, forced bites, food removal, or a fixed exposure quota? If so, ask for the rationale, risks, alternatives, and stopping rules.
- How do you coordinate with primary care, nutrition, mental health, the prescriber, and school?
- Do you need a referral or diagnosis?
- What are the costs, insurance requirements, waitlist, telehealth options, age range, and location limits?

## Signs a resource may not fit

Be cautious if a provider:

- promises a universal cure or guarantees a number of foods
- treats all food refusal as defiance
- dismisses choking, coughing, pain, vomiting, growth, hydration, or medication concerns
- recommends supplements, elimination diets, or medication changes without individualized assessment
- cannot explain what they assess, how they protect safety, or when they refer to another professional

## Keep directory entries current

For each organization, record:

- service name and direct link
- country, state, or region
- age range and service type
- informational, directory, clinical, or crisis role
- date last reviewed
- what a parent should verify before relying on it

Availability and scope change. A listing is not an endorsement.
