# Research and clinical-trial resources

Research registries can help families learn what is being studied. A registry entry is not proof that a study is safe, effective, current, or appropriate for a particular child. Do not stop treatment, delay urgent care, or change medication to qualify for a study.

## Official trial registries

- [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/) — international studies searchable by condition, age, location, and recruitment status.
- [WHO ICTRP Search Portal](https://trialsearch.who.int/) — international trial-registry search.
- [EU Clinical Trials Information System](https://euclinicaltrials.eu/ctis-public/search?lang=en) — EU/EEA medicinal-product trials.
- [FDA: Learn About Clinical Trials](https://www.fda.gov/patients/learn-about-expanded-access-and-other-treatment-options/learn-about-clinical-trials) — participation information and questions to ask.

## Search terms

Try synonyms such as **ADHD**, **attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder**, **ARFID**, **avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder**, **pediatric feeding disorder**, **feeding and swallowing**, **parent training**, or **sleep ADHD**. Check age, location, eligibility, recruitment status, study design, sponsor, risks, compensation, travel, and contact details.

## Questions before contacting a study team

- Is the study recruiting children in this age range and location?
- What are the known risks, burdens, alternatives, and visit requirements?
- Does participation replace any usual care, or can usual care continue?
- Who pays for travel, testing, treatment after the study, or unexpected complications?
- How will consent, child assent, privacy, and withdrawal work?

**Reviewed:** September 20, 2026. Registry details change; the registry and study team are the authoritative sources for current eligibility and participation information.
