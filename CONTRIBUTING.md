# Contributing

This repository is for careful, caregiver-friendly documentation—not diagnosis or individualized treatment plans.

## Source standards

- Prefer government health agencies, clinical practice guidelines, systematic reviews, peer-reviewed research, and established professional organizations.
- Record the publication or update date when available.
- Link directly to the source and note what claim it supports.
- Mark uncertainty, disagreement, and gaps instead of smoothing them over.
- Treat personal stories and forums as lived experience, not clinical evidence.
- Do not copy large passages. Summarize in your own words and retain links.

## Writing standards

- Use non-stigmatizing language and avoid describing a child as difficult, manipulative, or attention-seeking.
- Separate observation from interpretation: describe what happened, when, and in what setting.
- Include age range and location when a recommendation is jurisdiction-specific.
- Avoid rigid food rules, coercion, moral language about food, and unsupported supplement claims.
- Include a clinician discussion prompt when a topic could affect safety, growth, medication, or nutrition.

## Parent-first page contract

Every parent-facing page should answer, visibly and in this order unless safety requires an earlier warning:

1. What is this, and what does it not establish?
2. What can a parent try now, with purpose, scope, prerequisites, and stopping rules?
3. Who can help, what each role may assess, and what the role does not cover?
4. What questions can the parent take to a clinician, school, or service?
5. What are the emergency, prompt-care, and routine-escalation signs?
6. What are the dated sources, evidence or guidance type, jurisdiction, and limitations?

Prefer concern- and decision-based navigation over diagnosis-first navigation. A parent must be able to reach a safe next step without already knowing whether the child has ADHD, ARFID, pediatric feeding disorder, anxiety, or another condition.

## Action and service safety

For each suggested action, state its intended purpose, age or population scope, prerequisites, stopping rule, and escalation trigger. Do not make exposure, food linking, meal scheduling, reinforcement, sensory activities, supplements, elimination diets, nutritional products, medication changes, or feeding programs sound universal. Protect safe foods, hydration, growth, the child's dignity, and clinician-directed care.

When naming a professional or resource, explain what problem it may assess or coordinate, its limits, and what parents should verify about pediatric experience, feeding or swallowing competence, ARFID experience, referrals, insurance, waitlists, age range, and location.

Maintain one canonical red-flags page and link eating, swallowing, hydration, growth, medication, mental-health, and acute-distress pages to it. Separate emergency help from prompt clinical contact and routine follow-up.

## Citation and review checklist

Before committing a substantive page update:

- check that every material claim is supported by a source matching its age range, population, jurisdiction, and evidence type;
- distinguish research evidence, clinical guidance, government information, professional opinion, practical strategy, and lived experience;
- use unique citation identifiers within each document and remove duplicate or stale entries;
- state source limitations and review dates;
- check links and citation sections;
- run the citation ledger verification for new or materially changed sourced documents;
- run `git diff --check` and review the complete staged file list;
- check red-flag wording, jurisdiction, and privacy.

## Research notes

Use `templates/research-note.md` for new findings. Add a short entry to the relevant index only after checking the source and its scope. Never include identifying child details, school records, appointment histories, photographs, or private logs.
