# Food allergy, allergy anxiety, and fear-based restriction

Food allergy can require strict medical avoidance. It can also make a child fearful of eating or lead to a diet that becomes narrower than medically necessary. Allergy precautions and ARFID-related fear are different questions and may require coordinated care.[1][2]

## What to notice

Record confirmed allergies and the current action plan separately from foods avoided because of worry, past symptoms, texture, smell, or uncertainty. Note reactions, fear, school impact, and whether avoidance is expanding.

## What you can try now

- Follow the child’s allergist-provided action plan exactly.
- Keep medically necessary avoidance separate from unconfirmed internet-based restrictions.
- Offer reassurance without promising that a feared food is safe.
- Ask the child what they fear and write down their words for the care team.

Never reintroduce an allergen, change an emergency plan, or begin an elimination diet based on this website.

## Who to contact and what to ask

Ask the pediatrician or allergist about diagnosis, action plans, nutrition substitutions, and whether the child needs a dietitian or mental-health/feeding referral. Feeding and swallowing professionals may be part of coordinated care when skill or safety concerns are present.[3] Ask: “How do we protect allergy safety while addressing fear and expanding nutrition safely?”

## Escalate promptly

Use the child’s emergency allergy plan and local emergency services for a suspected severe allergic reaction. Seek prompt care for rapidly narrowing intake, dehydration, weight or growth concerns, repeated vomiting, or severe fear around eating.
