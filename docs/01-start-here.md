# Start here

## If you are worried about a child

Start with the child's pediatrician or primary care clinician. ADHD has no single diagnostic test, and sleep problems, anxiety, depression, learning differences, and other health conditions can look similar.[1][2] An evaluation usually needs information from more than one setting, such as home and school.[1][2]

For eating concerns, describe patterns rather than arguing over a label: foods avoided, safe foods, meal duration, distress, gagging or fear, hydration, constipation, energy, growth concerns, and what happens at school or social events. Ask whether the child needs evaluation by a pediatrician, registered dietitian, feeding specialist, psychologist/psychiatrist, or an eating-disorder service.

## Prepare for an appointment

Bring a one- or two-week factual log if it is safe and not burdensome:

- meals, snacks, and drinks accepted or refused
- sensory, fear, appetite, attention, or routine factors noticed
- symptoms such as gagging, pain, nausea, constipation, or fatigue
- school and social impact
- medication timing and appetite or sleep changes, if applicable
- questions and the child's own words

Do not turn the log into a calorie contest or use it to pressure the child. The goal is useful information for the care team.

## Questions worth asking

- What else could explain these symptoms?
- What measurements or evaluations are appropriate?
- Are growth, hydration, nutrient status, sleep, constipation, or swallowing concerns present?
- Which clinician has experience with both neurodevelopmental differences and feeding/eating concerns?
- What should we do if intake or health worsens?
- How will we monitor whether a treatment helps and whether side effects appear?

## How to use this repository

Read the [disclaimer](../DISCLAIMER.md) first. Then use the topic pages as question-generation tools, not as a self-diagnosis checklist. Check each link's date and jurisdiction. When sources conflict, keep both views visible and ask a qualified clinician to interpret them for the child.
