# Neurodevelopmental overlap and sensory differences

ADHD, autism, learning or language differences, sensory sensitivities, anxiety, and ARFID may overlap. Shared features such as rigidity, difficulty with transitions, sensory sensitivity, or restricted interests are not specific enough to diagnose any one condition.[1][2]

## What you can try now

- Make routines visible with pictures, words, timers, or advance notice.
- Reduce unnecessary noise, smells, visual clutter, and rushed transitions when those are barriers.
- Offer communication choices that let the child express “no,” “help,” “finished,” or “not yet.”
- Preserve safe foods and participation while the care team investigates the concern.

These are accommodations to test, not proof of a diagnosis or a replacement for assessment. Stop or change a support if it reduces eating, increases distress, or prevents the child from participating.

## Who to contact and what to ask

Ask the pediatrician about developmental, behavioral, learning, language, sleep, and feeding assessment. When feeding or swallowing safety is involved, ASHA recommends interprofessional care and clear role boundaries.[3] Ask prospective clinicians whether they have experience supporting neurodivergent children without using shame or compliance-only goals, and whether they can coordinate with feeding, mental-health, nutrition, and school professionals.
