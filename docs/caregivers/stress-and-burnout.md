# Caregiver stress, mental health, and burnout

Supporting a child through attention, feeding, sleep, school, or health difficulties can be exhausting. Parent strain is not evidence of failure, and “self-care” alone cannot solve inaccessible services, financial pressure, waitlists, or lack of respite.[1][2]

## What may help now

- Tell one trusted person what concrete help would reduce load: meals, transportation, childcare, calls, school communication, or appointment notes.
- Ask the child’s clinician about caregiver support, therapy, respite, social work, or care coordination.
- Use one shared notebook or folder for questions and appointments rather than carrying every detail mentally.
- Choose one sustainable home experiment at a time; a plan that overwhelms the caregiver is not a sustainable plan.

## A brief safety check

Seek immediate local crisis or emergency help if you feel unable to provide essential supervision, fear you may harm yourself or someone else, or are in immediate danger. In the United States, call or text 988 for emotional or suicide crisis support; use local services elsewhere.[3]

## What to ask the care team

- What can be simplified?
- Which tasks can another clinician, school professional, social worker, or support person own?
- What is the minimum safe plan while we wait for specialist care?
- What should happen if our family cannot follow the proposed plan as written?
