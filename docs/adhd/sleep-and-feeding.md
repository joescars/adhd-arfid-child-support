# Sleep, ADHD, and feeding

Sleep problems can affect attention, regulation, mood, learning, appetite, and family stress. They can also resemble or amplify ADHD symptoms. A sleep concern does not prove ADHD, and ADHD does not explain every sleep problem.[1][2]

## What to notice

For one to two weeks, if it is practical, note bedtime, time to fall asleep, waking, snoring or breathing pauses, morning functioning, daytime sleepiness, appetite, and medication timing. Record patterns rather than judging whether the child is “trying.”

## What you can try now

- Keep a predictable bedtime and wake time where feasible, including weekends.
- Reduce evening transitions and give advance notice before the routine changes.
- Use a short visual sequence such as wash, pajamas, quiet activity, bed.
- Keep screens and stimulating activities from crowding out sleep, using a plan the family can actually sustain.
- Protect enough time for meals and fluids; do not use hunger or food restriction as a sleep strategy.

These are low-risk routine supports, not a sleep-treatment program. Stop or simplify a change if it causes major distress, worsens eating, or makes the family routine less safe.

## Who to contact and what to ask

Start with the pediatrician or primary care clinician. Ask about snoring, breathing pauses, restless sleep, unusual daytime sleepiness, insomnia, pain, anxiety, medication effects, and whether a sleep evaluation is needed. NICE recommends assessing sleep patterns and sleep problems as part of ADHD care.[3]

Ask the prescriber: “Could the timing or side effects of this medication be affecting sleep or appetite, and what should we monitor?” Do not change medication, timing, supplements, or melatonin from this page.

## Escalate promptly when

Contact a clinician promptly for breathing pauses, loud or persistent snoring with daytime impairment, severe insomnia, unusual daytime sleepiness, major appetite or energy changes, or a sharp change after medication. Use [red flags and urgent help](../safety/red-flags-and-urgent-help.md) for emergency concerns.
