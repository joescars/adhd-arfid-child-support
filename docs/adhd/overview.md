# ADHD in children: an evidence-oriented starting point

ADHD is a developmental disorder involving persistent patterns of inattention, hyperactivity, and/or impulsivity that can interfere with daily life.[1] A diagnosis is a multi-step clinical process; there is no single test, and similar symptoms can arise from other conditions.[2]

## What a careful evaluation considers

A clinician may gather history, consider medical and developmental factors, use rating scales, and seek observations from adults who know the child in different settings.[1][2] The purpose is not only to count symptoms but to understand impairment, strengths, context, and possible co-occurring or alternative explanations.

## Support is usually broader than medication

For school-age children, guidance commonly discusses medication, behavior therapy or parent training, classroom interventions, organizational supports, and school collaboration.[3] Medication decisions require individualized prescribing and monitoring. Appetite and sleep changes are important to report, especially when eating is already difficult.[3]

## Caregiver observation prompts

- When is attention hardest: before meals, during meals, after school, or at bedtime?
- Does appetite vary with medication timing, stress, sleep, activity, or routine changes?
- Does the child miss hunger cues, forget to eat, become overwhelmed by choices, or avoid foods for sensory or fear-related reasons?
- What accommodations make school, meals, and transitions easier?
- Which strengths, interests, and successful routines can be built on?

These prompts are not diagnostic criteria. They are a way to make a clinician and school conversation more concrete.

## Practical guardrails

- Do not assume every eating difficulty is caused by ADHD.
- Do not stop or change medication without the prescriber.
- Ask the care team how appetite, growth, sleep, and nutrition will be monitored.
- Use short, clear directions and predictable routines as possible supports, not as proof that a child is choosing the difficulty.
