# Project instructions

This repository is documentation-first and health-related. Keep claims sourced, dated, and scoped to the population and jurisdiction studied. Do not add medical advice, diagnoses, private child information, credentials, or identifying records.

## Parent-first content contract

Every parent-facing page must visibly answer these questions, in this order unless safety requires an earlier warning:

1. **What is this?** Define the topic in plain language and state what it does not establish.
2. **What can a parent try now?** Give only low-risk, reversible supports. State the purpose, population, prerequisites, and stopping rule for each action.
3. **Who can help?** Name the likely first contact and relevant specialist types. Explain what each role may assess and its limits; availability and referral rules vary by location.
4. **What should a parent ask?** Provide concrete appointment, school, or care-coordination questions.
5. **Red flags and escalation.** Separate emergency signs from prompt clinical contact and routine follow-up. Link eating, swallowing, hydration, growth, medication, and acute-distress pages to the canonical red-flags page.
6. **Sources.** Map material claims to sources that match the age range, population, jurisdiction, evidence type, and claim scope. State limitations and review dates.

If a section does not apply, say why rather than silently omitting it. Prefer concern- and decision-based navigation over diagnosis-first navigation so a parent can find a safe next step without knowing whether the child has ADHD, ARFID, pediatric feeding disorder, anxiety, or another condition.

## Health and action safety

- Never diagnose a child from a checklist, webpage, or food preference.
- Do not recommend starting, stopping, or changing medication, supplements, restrictive diets, elimination diets, nutritional products, tube feeds, or medical treatment without individualized professional guidance.
- For every home or school strategy, state its intended purpose, age or population scope, prerequisites, stopping rules, and escalation trigger.
- Do not present exposure, food linking, meal scheduling, reinforcement, sensory activities, or feeding programs as universal treatment.
- Protect established safe foods, hydration, growth, dignity, the child's perspective, and clinician-directed care.
- Do not recommend unfamiliar textures or sizes when chewing or swallowing safety is uncertain.
- Treat refusal, avoidance, gagging, distress, and disruptive mealtime behavior as observations requiring curiosity—not proof of defiance or bad character.
- Keep emergency guidance jurisdiction-specific and current.

## Clinician and service navigation

When naming a clinician type, explain what problem that role may assess or coordinate and what it does not establish. Include questions parents can use to verify pediatric experience, ARFID or feeding-disorder experience, swallowing competence where relevant, team coordination, insurance or referral requirements, waitlists, age range, and local availability.

## Evidence and citations

Distinguish research evidence, clinical guidance, government information, professional opinion, practical caregiver strategy, and lived experience. Never convert a caregiver story or a practical suggestion into evidence of effectiveness. Verify that every citation supports the exact claim made; do not reuse ADHD sources for ARFID claims or general picky-eating guidance for older children with ARFID without an explicit scope note.

Use unique citation identifiers within each document, check that each identifier maps to one source only, remove duplicate or stale entries, and use a task-local ledger when strict verification would otherwise include unrelated project sources. Retrieve page content before drafting load-bearing claims; search snippets are discovery aids, not evidence.

## Privacy

Do not include identifiable child details, exact appointment histories, school records, photographs, or private logs in examples. Use fictionalized, non-identifying examples. The repository is public by default even while its GitHub visibility is private.

## Before committing documentation changes

- review the complete parent-facing page contract and source scope;
- check links and citation sections when practical;
- run citation-ledger verification for every new or materially changed sourced document;
- run `git diff --check`;
- review the complete staged file list;
- preserve the distinction between research evidence, clinical guidance, and lived experience;
- confirm red-flag wording, jurisdiction, and review dates.

Use the repository's `DISCLAIMER.md` and `CONTRIBUTING.md` as governing documents.
